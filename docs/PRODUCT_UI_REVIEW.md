# Product and web UI review

Reviewed October 20, 2026, against commit `23fbc7f`.

The next iteration should make everyday decisions easier: which raid needs an answer, what the answer means, which time applies, and whether the change is saved. Keep the charcoal, ivory, gold, and class-color identity.

**Evidence and limits**

Reviewed current source, existing usability plans, and rendered local screens: My raids, availability, characters, officer dashboard, planner schedule/roster, members, and settings. Used the existing 24-player fixture, three recurring raids, one published roster, and a fixed fixture clock. Screens were captured at 1440 × 1000 and 390 × 844; Home, availability, and planner were also checked at 320px. The viewer was in New York and the guild in Los Angeles to exercise overnight and timezone behavior.

Final captures mocked the backend client to null and used browser-only sample data with officer permissions. An earlier capture included a browser-guild warning and was replaced; the measurements below exclude it. Authenticated member behavior, Discord differences, and save behavior were reviewed in source, not verified against the live guild. Online-only briefings, history, and reminder panels were not exercised. These findings are a product review, not measured explanations of individual guild members' complaints or an accessibility certification.

No app code changed. The browser review exercised local roster publication, planner tabs, navigation, and expanding the availability editor. No uncaught page errors or horizontal overflow were observed in the sampled final captures. No application test suite was rerun for this documentation-only review.

**Five prioritized improvements**

1. **Make My raids revolve around the raid that needs an answer.** High impact; medium effort; high confidence in the UI issue. Home currently stacks a title-level availability action, section navigation, an action inbox, the next assignment, weekly availability, quick signup answers, upcoming raids, and a calendar. The same published raid can appear both as a confirmation and as a quick signup. Consolidate each raid into one member card with its date, character, current state, and appropriate action. Keep signup and roster confirmation as separate states, but present that progression in one place. Use language such as “Signed up · awaiting roster,” then “Selected · confirm your place,” then “Confirmed.” Those status concepts already exist. Make the next required action primary; keep the full availability editor secondary. Source: `src/views/Home.svelte:88`, `src/views/Home.svelte:112`, `src/views/Home.svelte:215`, `src/lib/raid-status.js`.

   First slice: move the confirmation action into the next-raid card, bring unanswered raids immediately below it, and remove duplicate quick answers for raids already represented by the assignment card. For fixed raid nights, allow routine answers without opening the full week editor. Success: members can identify their next raid, state, and required action in the first phone viewport; aim for a routine answer in two taps once a character is selected.

2. **Give web and Discord signups the same capabilities and rules.** High impact; medium-to-large effort; high confidence from source. Discord supports Sign up, Late, Tentative, Absent, per-raid spec choice, and signups after the weekly deadline until the raid starts. The web quick-answer control offers three answers, a character choice, and closes at the weekly deadline. Its RPC independently enforces that deadline. A member therefore gets different possibilities depending on where they respond. Source: `src/components/RaidQuickAnswer.svelte:19`, `src/components/RaidQuickAnswer.svelte:27`, `src/lib/raid-services.js:204`, `supabase/migrations/20261025120000_app_signups.sql:1`, and `supabase/functions/_shared/discord-interactions.js:305`.

   First slice: add an explicit Late answer and per-raid spec selection, show the saved character/spec/status from either channel, and align event-signup deadline policy with Discord. Keep weekly schedule editing and event-specific late changes distinct. This requires server changes as well as buttons. Success: the same member can perform the same raid response through either channel and see it reflected in the other, including after the weekly deadline and before raid start.

3. **Make the usual schedule and one-off exceptions the normal availability workflow.** High impact for repeat users; medium effort; high confidence in discoverability, adoption unmeasured. Repeating schedules, pauses, review prompts, away dates, and copying days already exist. Their discovery is the gap: “Usual schedule” sits below all seven days, approximately 1470px down in the phone fixture. Week edits, repeating schedules, all-raid time ranges, and per-raid character choices also have different scopes. Source: `src/views/Availability.svelte:361`, `src/components/UsualSchedule.svelte`, and `src/views/Availability.svelte:377`.

   First slice: after a first useful schedule, offer “Repeat these times each week.” For returning users, lead with the saved schedule summary and “Change this week,” “Change my usual schedule,” and “Mark away.” Preserve the existing pause/review controls under schedule management. Keep timezones and the effective week beside the edit. Success: a returning member with no schedule change does not need to reopen seven daily editors; one-off edits clearly state that future weeks are unchanged.

4. **Make a raid answer one reliable save with one visible result.** Medium-to-high impact; larger effort; high confidence in the split operation, frequency of failures unknown. Quick answers update check-in state, which enters the guild save queue, and separately invoke `signup_from_app` to update the signup list. A partial-failure toast and retry exist, but two independently saved records can temporarily disagree. This is an architectural risk, not a reproduced production incident. Source: `src/components/RaidQuickAnswer.svelte:38`, `src/components/RaidQuickAnswer.svelte:51`, `src/lib/raid-services.js:204`, and `supabase/migrations/20261025120000_app_signups.sql:1`.

   First slice: route event answers through one authorized server transaction that writes the check-in consequences and signup together, returns the canonical result, and queues Discord delivery. Make retries safe and display Pending / Saved / Retry on the affected raid card. The user should not need to reason about two saves. Existing row merging and recovery should be retained: `src/lib/remote/sync.svelte.js:733` already retries independent concurrent edits, and `src/lib/remote/rows.js:280` merges records. Success: failure-injection checks show either both raid-answer records update or neither does; retries do not create duplicate effects. Discord message delivery can remain asynchronous with a separate delivery status.

5. **Turn member feedback into a small, repeatable product feedback loop.** Medium impact; small initial effort; confidence depends on what members report. Error reporting exists, but the searched source has no task-completion instrumentation or in-app friction feedback. Runtime errors do not reveal a person spending a minute finding the right answer button. Source: `src/lib/error-reporting.js:31`.

   First slice: observe three to five members doing “sign up,” “change this Friday only,” and “find my roster place” without coaching. Record completion, hesitation, time, and device. Add a small “Something confusing?” entry that attaches the screen and viewport to user-written feedback, with no automatic capture of raid notes or personal messages. If ongoing metrics are needed, measure starts/completions/retries for those tasks with minimal data. Success: compare the same tasks before and after the first UI changes and prioritize recurring complaints by affected task.

**Implementation order and validation**

Start with selected-answer styling, the misleading Confirm shortcut, and clipped time inputs. Then simplify the Home/availability hierarchy and timezone presentation. Follow with server-backed web/Discord parity and the unified answer transaction. Observe member tasks before and after each meaningful UI slice.

Validate returning and first-time members, tentative/unavailable/selected/confirmed states, two characters with different specs, a raid crossing midnight, a DST transition, late changes after the weekly deadline, and an interrupted save. Check member and officer permissions separately. Keep keyboard focus, status announcements, zoom, and phone navigation usable.

W3C's [target-size guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html) defines the 24px AA minimum with exceptions; retain the app's larger 44px phone-action target as a design goal. Its [reflow guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html) supports checking 320 CSS pixels without two-direction scrolling where applicable. Its [status-message guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) supports announcing save/result changes without moving focus. The local screenshots alone do not establish conformance.
